1. Defining the Standards: UL 1310 Class 2 vs. UL 62368-1 HBSE

Both UL 1310 and UL 62368-1 are North American harmonized safety standards administered under the Nationally Recognized Testing Laboratory (NRTL) scheme (such as UL Solutions or Intertek ETL). However, their safety engineering philosophies, power envelopes, and downstream circuit requirements diverge significantly.

UL 1310: Standard for Class 2 Power Units

UL 1310 applies specifically to indoor and outdoor power supplies designed to connect to an alternating current (AC) branch circuit of 120V or 240V, delivering a Class 2 direct current (DC) output. The core concept of a Class 2 power unit, rooted in the US National Electrical Code (NEC Article 725), is inherent limitation of electrical shock and fire hazard.

Under UL 1310, the output voltage is strictly limited to 60V DC (under dry conditions) or 30V DC (wet/outdoor conditions), and the maximum continuous output power cannot exceed 100VA (approximately 100 Watts) under both normal operating and single-fault conditions. Because the energy available from the power supply is physically incapable of igniting common combustible materials or delivering a lethal electric shock, downstream devices (such as LED strips, smart home sensors, fountain pumps, and holiday lights) do not require fire-resistant enclosures or specialized field wiring. This makes our plug-in adapters and IP44 rainproof power supplies exceptionally popular among lighting and consumer hardware manufacturers.

UL 62368-1: Audio/Video, Information and Communication Technology Equipment

UL 62368-1 is the modern safety standard that completely superseded the legacy standards UL 60950-1 (for information technology equipment) and UL 60065 (for audio/video equipment) on December 20, 2020. Unlike its predecessors, UL 62368-1 is built upon Hazard-Based Safety Engineering (HBSE) principles.

UL 62368-1 does not impose a rigid 100W power ceiling. It classifies energy sources into three distinct tiers: Energy Source Class 1 (ES1/PS1 - safe to touch), Class 2 (ES2/PS2 - perceptible but not hazardous), and Class 3 (ES3/PS3 - capable of causing severe injury or fire). External power supplies delivering 65W, 90W, 120W, or up to 180W (such as our desktop power adapters for POS terminals, gaming peripherals, 3D printers, and industrial controllers) are certified under UL 62368-1. Safeguards are engineered to prevent energy transfer from hazardous circuits to accessible user touchpoints.

To assist component engineers, hardware architects, and sourcing directors in selecting the proper power supply topology, the structural differences are detailed below:

Comparison Dimension UL 1310 (Class 2 Power Units) UL 62368-1 (ICT / AV Safety Standard)
Regulatory Scope Fixed or cord-connected Class 2 power units providing isolated low-voltage DC power. Broad electronic safety standard for Audio/Video, Information & Telecom hardware.
Max Power & Voltage Strictly ≤ 100VA (≈100W); DC output voltage < 60V (dry) or < 30V (outdoor/wet). No inherent 100W ceiling; covers power ranges from 5W up to multiple kilowatts.

3. Mandatory North American Baseline: FCC Part 15B & DOE Level VI

A common mistake among first-time importers is believing that an NRTL safety mark (UL or ETL) is sufficient for market distribution. In reality, US federal law mandates electromagnetic compatibility and environmental energy efficiency compliance:

FCC Part 15 Subpart B (Unintentional Radiators)

Switching power supplies incorporate high-frequency Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) oscillator circuits operating between 65kHz and 500kHz. These switching harmonics naturally generate electromagnetic noise. Under Title 47 of the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR), all external power adapters must comply with FCC Part 15 Subpart B:

  • Class B Limits (Residential & Commercial): Designed for equipment intended for use in homes and offices. Emission thresholds are significantly more stringent than Class A to prevent interference with broadcast television, cellular signals, and Wi-Fi networks. All consumer power adapters manufactured by Hua Yi Teng are engineered to meet and exceed Class B emission standards.
  • Supplier's Declaration of Conformity (SDoC): SDoC requires the responsible party in the US to retain a comprehensive test report from an accredited testing laboratory proving radiated and conducted emissions pass within limits.

DOE Level VI / CEC / NRCan Energy Efficiency

Since February 2016, the US Department of Energy (DOE) has enforced strict Level VI efficiency standards for external power supplies (EPS). Compliance requires:

  • Active Mode Efficiency: The four-point average efficiency (measured at 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100% of rated output) must exceed calculated statutory thresholds, often demanding >88% to 92% efficiency for mid-to-high wattage adapters.
  • No-Load Standby Power: Unloaded quiescent power consumption must remain below 0.1W (or 0.075W for smaller units), dramatically minimizing parasitic grid drain.
  • Regulatory Filings: Importers must maintain filings with the US DOE Compliance Certification Management System (CCMS) and California Energy Commission (CEC Title 20), as well as Natural Resources Canada (NRCan).

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a UL 62368-1 certified power supply directly replace a UL 1310 Class 2 power supply?

Not automatically. UL 1310 enforces inherent output power and energy limits (maximum 100VA / 100W, dry-location voltage below 60VDC) such that connected end-devices and wiring are exempt from secondary electrical fire and shock containment requirements under the National Electrical Code (NEC). A UL 62368-1 power supply might output higher energy levels (PS2 or PS3), requiring the downstream device chassis and wiring to provide certified fire enclosure protection. However, if a UL 62368-1 power supply is specifically engineered and certified with an evaluated Limited Power Source (LPS) output, it can satisfy Class 2 circuit boundaries for most consumer applications.

What are Amazon US compliance audit requirements for AC/DC power adapters regarding FCC and UL?

Amazon North America strictly polices power adapters under Category Guidelines. Sellers must submit: (1) An ISO 17025 accredited laboratory test report showing compliance with UL 1310 or UL 62368-1 (or an OSHA NRTL certificate such as UL or ETL Listing); (2) An FCC Supplier's Declaration of Conformity (SDoC) test report proving compliance with FCC Part 15 Subpart B (Class B residential limits); (3) Legible photos of product rating labels displaying the NRTL safety mark, FCC ID/logo, model number, and manufacturer details; (4) Proof of DOE Level VI energy efficiency registration with the US Department of Energy compliance certification database (CCMS). Failure to supply these documents within 30 days results in immediate listing suppression and FBA inventory removal.
